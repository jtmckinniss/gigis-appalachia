Peer-reviewed · 2026 · Gigi's original research

L.I.F.E.™ — a better way to see how shelter dogs are doing

In plain terms

Most shelter behavior evaluations put a dog through artificial challenges and hope the reaction predicts real life — an approach with mixed results. L.I.F.E. (Living Interactions, Functionality Evaluation™) takes the opposite path: it watches how a dog actually lives in the shelter — eating, resting, interacting — to identify the dogs who are struggling to cope and need help now. This paper is the tool's formal development and validation, so any shelter can use it with confidence.

What we found

Validated across 800+ shelter dogs.

Trained observers without behavior credentials scored dogs nearly identically to the experts — meaning any shelter can assess how its dogs are coping, with or without a behaviorist on staff.

The citation

Herron ME, Martin A, Dowling-Guyer S. Development and Validation of an Observational Behavior Tool (LIFE™) for Assessing Canine Coping in Shelter Environments. Animals. 2026;16(14):2182. doi:10.3390/ani16142182

Changing when puppies enter the shelter saves lives

In plain terms

Parvovirus hits puppies hardest, and the risk concentrates where young, unprotected puppies crowd together. This community case study asked a simple question: what happens if you change when puppies enter the shelter system? The answer: fewer infections and more survivors — not from a new drug or a new building, but from a protocol change other shelters can adopt.

What we found

Parvo infections fell from 47% to 9%.

After intake timing changed, fewer than 1 in 10 puppies contracted parvovirus — down from nearly half. Survival among infected puppies trended up too (72% to 89%), though the smaller numbers make that part less certain.

The citation

Hadden H, Herron M. Effects of Deferred Puppy Intake on Incidence of Canine Parvovirus Infection and Survival: A Community Case Study. Journal of Shelter Medicine and Community Animal Health. 2025;4(1). doi:10.56771/jsmcah.v4.109

Peer-reviewed · 2024

Real-world evidence for a medication vets already rely on

In plain terms

Gabapentin is one of the most commonly prescribed medications for anxious and fearful dogs — but the evidence behind that everyday use has been thin. This study went back through real patient records to document how dogs actually responded, giving veterinarians firmer footing for a treatment they already reach for.

What we found

72% of families saw real improvement.

Across 50 dogs' records, nearly three in four families rated gabapentin moderately or very effective. Side effects — most often sleepiness — were common but manageable, so finding the right dose is an individual process.

The citation

Kirby-Madden T, Waring CT, Herron M. Effects of Gabapentin on the Treatment of Behavioral Disorders in Dogs: A Retrospective Evaluation. Animals. 2024;14(10):1462. doi:10.3390/ani14101462

Published abstract · 2025

A new parvo treatment, tested where it matters

In plain terms

A new monoclonal-antibody treatment for parvovirus arrived with real promise — but shelter dogs aren't laboratory dogs. This abstract reports early findings from using the treatment on naturally infected dogs in a working shelter setting: the kind of real-world evidence the field needs before adopting a therapy widely.

What we found

Recovery in half the time.

Across 112 shelter dogs, those given the antibody treatment cleared the virus faster and left isolation in a median of 2 days instead of 4 — the first evidence of this treatment's clinical efficacy and safety in a real shelter population.

The citation

Welton M, Hadden H, Herron ME, Winston JA. Clinical Effects of Canine Parvovirus Monoclonal Antibody Treatment in Naturally Occurring Parvovirus Infection in Sheltered Dogs [Abstract]. Journal of Shelter Medicine and Community Animal Health. 2025;4(S1). doi:10.56771/jsmcah.v4.141

Conference research · 2025

Making life-saving transport less stressful

In plain terms

Transport saves dogs' lives — but the ride itself is stressful. This study tested whether a calming supplement derived from a milk protein, layered on top of a stress-minimizing transport protocol, measurably lowers stress for dogs moving between organizations.

The citation

Wilkinson-Kenner C, Feuerbacher E, Spaulding K, Craig C, Herron ME. Effects of an Alpha-Casozepine Based Supplement, in Addition to a Stress Minimizing Transport Protocol, on Reducing Stress in Dogs During Inter-Organizational Transport. Veterinary Behavior Symposium, research presentation. 2025.
